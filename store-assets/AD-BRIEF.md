# Animated ad brief — What's My Pick!

Everything here is checked against what the app actually does (`src/components/mystery/`,
`src/lib/wordLists.js`, `supabase/migrations/`), so nothing in it can trip Apple's or
Google's "misleading claims" rules. Anything you add on top of this, verify the same way.

The two existing mascots (`mascot-blue.webp`, `mascot-red.webp`) are the cast. They are
already 3D, expressive and on-brand — the ad should feel like those two came to life.

---

## 1. The game in one line

> Everyone secretly picks a word. Now guess theirs — before they guess yours.

Three-line version, for a voiceover or a landing page:

> Everyone picks a secret word from the same category.
> You ask the whole room one yes/no question at a time — and every single player
> answers about their own word.
> The clues pile up. Someone is about to be caught. Make sure it isn't you.

---

## 2. Full description (the animator's bible)

**What's My Pick!** is a real-time social deduction word game for **2 to 12 players**.
Everyone is hiding something at the same time, and everyone is hunting at the same time.

**Don't call it "twenty questions" in the ad.** There is no question limit — the game runs
until one secret is left. The phrase makes people expect a rule that isn't there.

The loop, exactly as it plays:

1. **The room.** One player creates a game and shares a short room code, or hosts a
   public lobby anyone can drop into. No account, no signup — type a name and you're in.
2. **The category.** The room agrees on one category: Pets, Actors, Movies, Football
   Players, European Countries, Household Objects — or a custom one they type themselves.
3. **The secret.** A **60-second** clock starts and everyone privately picks their word
   from that category. Nobody sees anybody else's. This is the moment the whole game
   hangs on — a dozen people committing to a secret against a shared timer.
4. **The turn.** On your turn you have **30 seconds** to ask the entire room one yes/no
   question. *"Is it something you'd find in a kitchen?"* Stall too long and the game
   asks a question **for** you, out of its own question bank — your turn never dies quietly.
5. **The answers.** Every other player answers **about their own secret word**. One
   question, up to eleven different answers, all of them true, all of them useful.
   Players have **45 seconds** to answer before the round moves on.
6. **The notebook.** Every answer is filed per player in your notebook. It is a live grid
   of who said yes to what — the visual proof that you are closing in on someone.
7. **The guess.** Any time, you can call out one player and name their word. Right: you
   score, and their secret is revealed to the whole room. Wrong: everyone now knows what
   you were thinking, and that word is crossed off your list forever.
8. **The hint round.** Every five full rounds the game stops dead and forces *everyone* to
   type a hint about their own word — up to 120 characters. It's the pressure valve that
   turns a stalemate into a bloodbath.
9. **The end.** The game runs until only one secret is left unguessed. Points decide the
   winner; the last word standing decides the bragging rights. Then the room fires off a
   share card and hits Play Again.

Around the match: **Picks** and levels earned purely by playing, **100+ unlockable
cosmetics** (emblems, banners, borders, titles, name colours, Common through Mythic),
daily rewards, challenges and a win streak. Chat with emotes for cheering, bluffing and
winding each other up. A full **offline practice match against bots** if nobody's online.
Fully playable in **English and Dutch**.

## 3. The catchy parts — ranked by how well they sell in an ad

These are the beats that make somebody stop scrolling. Use them in this order.

**1. One question, everybody answers — differently.**
This is *the* hook and it is uniquely visual. Blue asks *"Is it alive?"* and the screen
splits into six faces answering at once: YES. NO. YES. NO. NO. YES. Nobody else's game
does this. Lead with it. It explains the whole product in two seconds without a word of
narration.

**2. The moment someone gets caught.**
Red narrows their eyes, points across the table, names the word — and Blue's secret card
flips face-up in front of everyone. Confetti, emoji rain, the works. This is the payoff
shot and it is the one people replay.

**3. The wrong guess.**
Just as good as the right one, and funnier. Red says it with total confidence. The buzzer
hits. And now the whole room knows exactly what Red was thinking. Comedy *and* a mechanic
in one beat.

**4. The clocks.**
A visible timer draining while a mascot sweats and blurts out something terrible. Urgency
you can see from across a room, on mute, at 1x speed. Two clocks are worth showing: the
**60 seconds** to lock in your secret at the start, and the **30 seconds** to ask on your turn.

**5. The auto-question.**
Time runs out, the game asks a question on your behalf, and it's *good* — and the asker's
face falls because it just helped everybody else. A perfect three-frame joke.

**6. The hint round.**
Five rounds in, everything freezes and every player is forced to give something away.
Show six mascots typing at once with a panicked look. "Everyone has to bleed a little."

**7. The notebook.**
The detective shot. Pull in on a grid of yes/no ticks per opponent, one column narrowing
to a single possibility, and the mascot's eyes going wide. This is what makes the game
look *smart* rather than random — important for the players who stay.

**8. "No account. Type a name. Play."**
Save this for the end card. It is the single biggest reason someone actually installs
after watching, and it kills the last objection right before the CTA.

**9. 2 to 12 players.**
Show the table physically growing — two mascots, then four, then a dozen crammed in.
Communicates "this works at your party" instantly.

## 7. Accuracy guardrails

Do not let the animation imply anything the app doesn't do:

- **Players don't lie.** Everyone answers honestly about their own word — the tension is
  in what they *don't* say. Never animate a mascot giving a false answer.
- **Nobody gets knocked out for guessing wrong.** A wrong guess costs you information,
  not your seat. Players only leave by leaving.
- **The bots are honest too.** They answer from a hand-authored fact table, so "bots that
  answer honestly about their own word" is literally true. Don't call them AI opponents.
- **Cosmetics are earned, never sold.** Picks and every cosmetic come from playing. Only
  the optional category packs are purchasable. Never show a currency purchase.
- **Free categories are Pets, Actors, Movies, Football Players, European Countries,
  Household Objects.** If you show a word from Fantasy, Food, Pop Culture, Brands, Animal
  or World, it belongs to an optional pack — don't frame it as included.
- **No "#1", "best", no competitor names.** Both stores reject listings for that.
- **Don't invent a question limit.** No "20 questions", no "you get 5 guesses". The only
  hard numbers are the timers (60s to pick, 30s to ask, 45s to answer, 60s hint round),
  2–12 players, and the hint round every 5 full rounds.
- **This is not an App Store app preview.** Apple requires every frame of an App Store
  preview to be device-captured footage of the app itself, 15–30s. An animated ad is for
  paid social, YouTube and the Play listing's promo video — not that slot.
- **The chat is user-to-user**, which is why the rating is 13+. Keep any on-screen chat
  content mild.
